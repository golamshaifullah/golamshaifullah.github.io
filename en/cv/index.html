My research spans precision pulsar timing, pulsar astrophysics and nanohertz gravitational-wave science. My early work established how measurement uncertainty, instrumentation and propagation effects enter pulsar-timing experiments, combining long-baseline analysis with an invited review that placed these effects in a common experimental framework.

I subsequently moved from individual timing experiments to international PTA datasets and gravitational-wave inference. I coordinated the EPTA DR2 timing dataset, contributed independent searches and statistical validation for its gravitational-wave background analysis, and developed reproducible high-performance-computing environments and researcher training.

My current programme tests the robustness of PTA inference under realistic statistical, computational and astrophysical conditions. It encompasses cross-sampler validation, realistic supermassive-black-hole-binary injection and recovery, accelerated Bayesian computation, observing-strategy studies, and the interpretation of nanohertz signals in terms of astrophysical and cosmological source populations. Much of this work is carried out by researchers I supervise.

Alongside PTA science, I study pulsars across a broad range of radio frequencies using Arecibo, Effelsberg, the Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope, Parkes, LOFAR, NenuFAR and MeerKAT. This work includes pulsar astrophysics, using pulsars to probe the heliosphere and coronal mass ejections, and searches for dark matter with pulsar polarisation arrays.
