Golam Mohiuddin Shaifullah

Postdoctoral researcher in the G. Occhialini Department of Physics at the University of Milano-Bicocca. I am a radio astronomer studying pulsars: the rapidly rotating, collapsed remnants of supernovae. I use these objects to search for nanohertz gravitational waves and to study ionised gas in the Milky Way and the Solar system.

Research

My research centres on creating and analysing high-precision pulsar-timing datasets. Pulsar timing arrays use pulsars distributed across the Galaxy to detect very-low-frequency gravitational waves, including signals produced by inspiralling supermassive black-hole binaries. I combine observations from telescopes and instruments used by the European and International Pulsar Timing Arrays, study propagation through the interstellar medium, and improve models that mitigate those effects.

I also study pulsars over a broad range of radio frequencies using facilities including Arecibo, Effelsberg, the Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope, Parkes, LOFAR, NenuFAR and MeerKAT.
